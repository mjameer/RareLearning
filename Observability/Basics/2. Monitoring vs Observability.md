Monitoring is a component of the broader concept of observability.

**Monitoring** involves tracking known, pre-defined metrics (like CPU usage) to tell you *when* and *where* an issue occurs. This approach is suited for simpler, monolithic systems where failure points are anticipated.

**Observability** is better suited for modern, complex, and distributed systems (like microservices). It provides a holistic view by gathering and analyzing diverse data from the entire system to tell you *why* an issue occurred. This allows you to anticipate and diagnose "unknown unknowns"—problems you didn't know to look for in advance.

The text uses a car analogy:
* **Monitoring:** Your car's dashboard, which tells you the "check engine" light is on (the "what," "when," and "where").
* **Observability:** The mechanic's diagnostic tool, which pulls detailed data to find the root cause (the "why").
